The Comms Board

What has this person actually been sent?

One question, which nothing in the programme could answer until this existed.
Not what we meant to send. Not what their milestones imply we sent. What reached them.
The problem

The answer was in three places, none of them queryable

Before the log existed, "has this founder been told?" required opening three different things and reconciling them by eye. None could be searched together, and none of them knew about the others.

Hardcoded arrays inside each sender

An ALREADY_SENT list in the route itself, edited by hand after every batch so the next run would skip people. Correct only until somebody forgot.

A "sent" flag in one sheet tab

Covering one email type, for one audience, with no date on it.

Markdown send logs

For the batches that went out by hand from Gmail. Real records, in prose, unqueryable.

Every row is one email to one person. A send to a founder and their mentor on the same thread writes two rows, because the question being asked is always about a person, never about a message. Which is why the board is addressed by participant rather than by campaign.

The column that mattered most

How we know is not the same as how sure we are

A tick in a comms board is a claim about the past, and the claims are not equally good. One is a receipt written by the thing that did the sending. The rest are reconstructions made afterwards, from evidence of varying quality.

Collapsing those into one green tick would have made the board confident and wrong, which is worse than a board that admits what it is guessing at. So there are five provenances, and only one of them is a receipt.

ProvenanceWhat it meansTrusted?
Logged at send Written by the route that sent it, at the moment it sent. The only true receipt. Yes
Backfilled from the sender's list Recovered from the hardcoded skip-list a route was carrying. The route did send them. Yes
Backfilled from the sheet Recovered from the old "sent" flag. A record, just not a dated one. Yes
Reconstructed from Gmail Found in a mailbox after the fact. Evidence something went, not proof of what. Shown softer
Recorded by hand Somebody wrote it down. Usually true, occasionally optimistic. Shown softer
The Comms tab shows them differently so a reconstructed tick is never mistaken for a receipt. Five provenances, two confidence levels, one visual difference that carries the whole distinction.
The row that reads 0 / 68

Showing the email you have not sent

"Meet your cohort" is written. It tells a founder which of the five peer rooms they are in, who else is in it, and why those people. It has a template, a subject and a render. There is no sender route for it, so it has never gone out.

The easy thing is to leave an unsendable email off the board. It would look finished. Instead the row sits there reading 0 / 68 with a drafted, never sent tag, because that is the single most useful fact the board holds: the one thing the programme has written for founders and not delivered.

What the log cannot tell you, and says so

The board answers what the routes sent. It is not evidence that a person was never contacted, because plenty of conversations happen by hand from a mailbox and never touch a route at all.

An empty row means "no send is recorded", not "nobody spoke to them". Getting that distinction into the interface mattered more than the feature did: a comms board that quietly implies silence will have someone chasing a founder who has already been called.

The bit I would defend hardest

An email that asks something becomes answerable by being written down

Six of the fourteen emails put a question to the person. Those answers need recording against the email that asked, so "we asked and they said yes" is a fact the programme holds rather than something living in one person's memory.

The obvious implementation is a list of answerable email keys somewhere in the admin board. It works, and it is a trap: every new email that asks something then requires a second edit in a second place, and the day someone forgets, the question silently stops being answerable.

So asks is a property of the email itself, in the catalogue. Writing the email down is the whole of the work. Nothing has to remember to keep up.

A new email that asks something should become answerable by being written down once, not by remembering to add its key in a second place. The same rule the rest of the system runs on, applied to the thing that is easiest to get lazy about.
