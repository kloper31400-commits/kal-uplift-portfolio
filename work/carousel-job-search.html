How I'd Apply
For A Job Today

A founder at last night's meetup asked me how I'd go about it. Turns out I have a framework. It's not foolproof, but it works.

12 steps to an advocate
Kennedy Loper Swipe
Start here

The interview process is where companies decide whether to hire you.

Before that, they have to decide whether you're worth talking to.

Here's how I'd get that first conversation.

01 / The setup Swipe
1
Step one

Find an advocate

Hiring managers get a sh*t ton of email. Trust me. Look for multiple ways to be seen.

▸Someone senior enough to forward your email
▸Someone on the team with a similar background, or whose career path resembles yours
▸Even the general inbox, if it raises the odds someone passes it on

Email formats are predictable

Most companies use one of three, all on @company.com

firstname firstname.lastname flastname

One inbox is not a strategy. Build twenty doors.

02 / Find an advocate Swipe
2
Step two

Become a familiar name

Follow the company. Pay attention to what they're building. Engage when you genuinely have something to add.

The quiet advantage

Company pages get far less engagement than personal profiles, so your comment actually gets noticed there.

▸It's a weird psychological thing: if I've seen your name before, it already feels like a little alignment
▸You're no longer a complete stranger

Show up before you ask for anything.

03 / Become a familiar name Swipe
3
Step three

Make me feel like you care

Spray and pray is the going strategy. I get it, it's partly a numbers game. But if I'm spending my time advocating for you, I want to believe you actually care.

Reference something specific

I don't need this to be your dream company. I do need to believe you cared enough to understand what we're building.

▸A recent launch
▸A product
▸A customer
▸Something the person recently shared
▸A problem you think we're trying to solve

One specific sentence beats four polished paragraphs that could have gone to anyone.

04 / Make me feel like you care Swipe
4
Step four

Build the job you want

Don't make me guess where you fit. Tell me.

▸What you're exceptionally good at
▸The problem you think you can help solve
▸The value you believe you could create
▸What that ROI might look like

The best roles aren't always found. Sometimes they're built around someone who makes their value obvious.

05 / Build the job you want Swipe
5
Step five

Make your email forwardable

If someone likes your message, there's a good chance they'll forward it. Write it so it still makes sense without you there.

Five questions it must answer

I don't want to write three paragraphs of context to pass you along.

▸Who are you?
▸Why this company?
▸What are you good at?
▸Why are you reaching out?
▸What are you asking for?

Attach the résumé. Include the LinkedIn. Build the full picture.

But don't overload me. Being concise is a skill too.
06 / Make your email forwardable Swipe
A note on tone

Don't over-polish it

Over-polished reads as green. Corporate lingo makes everyone a little nauseous.

Too casual

✗"Sup, you guys hiring?"
✗"Just wanted to pick your brain"

Too corporate

✗"Hope this finds you well"
✗"Looking forward to connecting"

About right

✓"Hey Kennedy" is fine. Start like a human.
✓"Saw you shipped X. I built the same thing at Y."
✓"I can help with Z, and here's the reason."
✓"Here's an event you'd like." Or an article you actually read.

Write like you'd talk to a colleague you respect. Then say the actual thing.

07 / A note on tone Swipe
6
Step six

Optimize your résumé for the first 15 seconds

When I open your PDF, I'm not reading. I'm scanning for signals.

▸Titles
▸Skills
▸Career progression
▸Results, or anything that immediately stands out

Then hand it over properly

✓One page. Clear formatting. No treasure hunt.
✓A PDF, not a Doc. Name it like a person would:
firstname-lastname-resume.pdf

Your résumé isn't trying to tell your whole story. It's trying to earn another 15 seconds.

08 / Your résumé in 15 seconds Swipe
7
Step seven

Treat LinkedIn like part of the application

If your résumé catches my attention, I'm almost always clicking your profile next. That's where I get context a résumé can't give me.

What I'm actually looking for

▸What you're interested in
▸What kinds of problems you think about
▸What you've built
▸How you communicate
▸What you're curious about

Your résumé says what you've done. Your profile says how you think.

09 / LinkedIn is the application Swipe
8
Step eight

Give people something to remember

I usually include one or two human details in my own outreach. Maybe you…

▸Lived abroad
▸Host monthly dinners
▸Coach a team
▸Built something weird out of curiosity
▸Have an unexpected hobby

Save the personality for your outreach and your LinkedIn. Keep the résumé on competence.

I don't want your CV reading like your MySpace page.
10 / Be memorable Swipe
9
Step nine

Reduce risk

Every part of your application is answering the same question:

"Would I feel comfortable putting my reputation behind this person?"

That's what hiring really is. You're asking someone inside the company to spend a little of their social capital on you.

The easier you make that call, the better your odds.
11 / Reduce risk Swipe
10
Step ten

Make it ridiculously easy to say yes

Everything below is friction

✗Every extra click
✗Every missing link
✗Every unanswered question
✗Every vague sentence

So remove it

✓Attach the résumé
✓Include your LinkedIn
✓Spell out the value you bring
✓Suggest a clear next step

People rarely say no because something is impossible. They say no because it takes a little too much effort.

12 / Easy to say yes Swipe
11
Step eleven

Be respectfully persistent

Most people send one email, then disappear. But persistence isn't more check-ins. It's progress.

Follow up with progress, not pings

One "just checking in" is plenty. After that, only come back when you have something you actually want to share.

▸You shipped something. Learned something. Hit a number. Built the thing you said you'd build.
▸Change up the format too: a comment on their post, a DM, something you published, a demo link
▸Every touch should give them something, not ask them for something

Persistence isn't annoying. Lack of self-awareness is. There's a difference.

Don't confuse silence with rejection.
13 / Be respectfully persistent Swipe
12
Step twelve

Remember what you're actually trying to do

The goal isn't to get hired. The goal is to get someone inside the company excited enough to bet a little of their reputation on you.

Nothing here gets you the job

▸All of it gets you an advocate
▸An advocate gets you the conversation
▸The conversation gets you the opportunity

Because before anyone decides whether to hire you, someone has to decide you're worth introducing.
