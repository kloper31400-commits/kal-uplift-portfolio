## EVENT DESCRIPTION

We put out a call for founders building with AI. More than 100 companies raised
their hand in under 24 hours.

We had three spots.

On Thursday, September 10, those three companies take the stage at Apprentice.IO,
a TechUnited community member, in Jersey City. Doors at 6:30, pitches at 7:00.
Five minutes to pitch. Five minutes of live Q&A, straight from the room.

### Who is pitching

**CLICR**
Harrison Wilkins, Co-founder and CEO, with co-founder and COO Lucas Martinez

Venues and events still run on analog counters, radios, and text threads. CLICR
replaces all of it with counters that communicate and one live dashboard:
real-time occupancy, foot traffic, and crowd analytics a team can check from
anywhere and compare over time. They have counted more than 1.6 million guests,
powered live crowd data for TikTok events, and ran all 215 events of Atlanta
Tech Week 2026. Know your crowd. Grow your revenue.

Backed by HCP Ventures. Their pilots, partnerships, and active user testing
include Grand Central Station, TikTok, House of Yes, The Hotel Chelsea, Electric
Shuffle, and SXSW.

**mobius**
Caroline Turnbull, Co-founder and CEO, with co-founder and CTO Julia Suozzi

The social network for fitness centered around community, not metrics. Share
your walks, your runs, your yoga class, and whatever type of movement you love
with a community that cheers it all on equally. On mobius, all movement counts.

Caroline is an engineer, trainer, yoga teacher, and former D1 athlete. Julia is
a software engineer and certified personal trainer who believes walks count too.
The app is live on the App Store, they run their own events in New York, and
they are already selling newsletter sponsorships.

**Juricratic**
Connor O'Dea, Founder, CEO and CTO

Most of legal AI right now is busy improving the workflows that already exist.
Juricratic is working several innovation cycles past that, building what Connor
calls an entirely different class of legal intelligence infrastructure. Not a
wrapper, not a copilot, not a productivity tool. The public description stays
deliberately thin, because the architecture is the product: years of research,
mathematics, and engineering compounding into a system. In his words, features
can be copied and systems are much harder to replicate. That is the moat.

Connor is a Berkeley-trained AI and ML systems architect who had his first exit
at 24, building with a team that combines technical, legal, and academic depth.
