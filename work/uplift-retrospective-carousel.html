What Uplift is

Uplift pairs women and minority founders with experienced mentors for 10 weeks: one-on-one mentorship, practical workshops, and in-person events. Free for founders, run by TechUnited.

and this summer?
The biggest Uplift program ever.
five groups of founders at once
8,000+ mentorship minutes
one summer

How did a small team pull that off, and what did we learn? That's this document.

Uplift Retrospective02 / 15
how we ran it

We treat community programs like products:

You ship.
You measure.
You listen.
You improve.

And every single improvement we shipped had the same goal:

Increase the surface area for meaningful relationships.

More chances for the right people to meet. More time for our team to be human with founders. The pages that follow are the six big things we tried, each with its so-what.

Uplift Retrospective03 / 15
What we tried · No. 1five groups at once
What we did

Uplift usually runs as one group of founders. This summer we ran five in parallel, each with its own sessions, events, and identity.

so what?
Founders stopped identifying with their group and started identifying with Uplift.

Ideas traveled between groups. Mentors met founders beyond the ones they were assigned. One community, five front doors. Would I do it again? Yes.

→ more surface area for relationships.
Uplift Retrospective04 / 15
What we tried · No. 2interactive stations
What we did

At our in-person events, we replaced awkward, open-ended icebreakers with stations that shepherd conversations:

a thank-you card table → set the tone for how we approach the room

networking bingo → practice asking people for things

a 60-second pitch booth → more founders seen, heard, and talked about

so what?
People don't need networking events. They need permission to start conversations.

Notice what didn't happen: "share a fun fact," "go meet more people." Every station gave people something to do together. We obsess over reducing friction in products and activations. A live community runs on the same fundamentals.

→ more surface area for relationships.
Uplift Retrospective05 / 15
What we tried · No. 3monday meetups
What we did

In our one-on-one check-ins, founders told us directly: "we want more opportunities to engage with our peers." We were transparent back: we didn't have the bandwidth for more programming. So we proposed something simpler. What if we all just agreed to meet on Mondays? Would you come?

they said yes. and it was awesome.

so what?
Big events create moments. Small rituals create relationships.

We all know this lesson. The connection hot spots surface around big events, but big events take effort and arrive rarely. Mondays cost almost nothing and come every week. Founders kept showing up. It's now permanent.

→ more surface area for relationships.
Uplift Retrospective06 / 15
What we tried · No. 4smarter mentor matching
What we did

Every Uplift founder is paired with a mentor. This summer an algorithm proposed the pairings, based on industry, stage, and what each founder needed. Our team made every final call.

so what?
The strongest mentor matches in program history.

Many of those pairs plan to keep meeting after the program ends. That says everything.

→ more surface area for relationships.
Uplift Retrospective07 / 15
What we tried · No. 5AI on the back office
What we did

We let AI handle the admin: attendance tracking, session summaries, progress reports, reminders. (Imperfectly. It broke sometimes.)

so what?
Every hour not spent on admin became an hour with founders.

That reclaimed bandwidth is what paid for Monday meetups and the extra in-person programming. AI didn't make the program less personal. It made it more personal.

→ more surface area for relationships.
Uplift Retrospective08 / 15
What we tried · No. 6the founder portal
What we did

We built each founder a simple portal: their sessions, their progress, and exactly what was left to finish the program.

so what?
The team could finally see every founder's journey at a glance.

"Who needs help this week?" went from guesswork to a glance. That changes how you support people.

→ more surface area for relationships.

Uplift Retrospective10 / 15
the surprise we didn't plan
What happened

As the summer went on, the ecosystem started showing up for our founders: Plug and Play, EqualSpace, Golden Seeds, investors, even the Mayor of Hoboken.

so what?
One introduction leads to another. One person who shows up brings the next. That's how an ecosystem compounds.

None of it came from marketing. It came from people talking about the founders.

→ more surface area for relationships.

now, the honest part
What broke

Not everything worked. Messages slipped through the cracks. Our home-built backend broke in places. And we focused on the founder experience more than the bigger home we were building: mentors deserve that same support, so the next version expands to include them.

so what?
You can't improve systems that have never been tested.

Running five groups at once stress-tested everything we had. Honestly? I'm grateful. Every rough edge is now a line on the next version's list.

Uplift Retrospective13 / 15
the takeaways · ten principles, part one
  1. Communities are architected.Every warm moment this summer traces back to a decision: who got matched, which station sat where, what happened on Mondays. Design the conditions and the connection follows.
  2. Community scales through systems.A small team supported five groups because matching, tracking, and reporting ran on systems. The hours those systems saved went to people.
  3. The best networking is designed.A thank-you table, bingo, a pitch booth. Each gave people something specific to do together, and the conversations started themselves.
  4. Relationships compound across groups.Our strongest bonds crossed group lines. Every founder, mentor, and partner added raises the odds that the right two people meet.
  5. Automation should buy back human time.Every hour AI saved on admin went into a one-on-one, a Monday meetup, or an in-person event. That's the test for adding any of it.
Uplift Retrospective14 / 15
the takeaways · part two
  1. Small rituals beat big events.Coffee on Mondays did more for relationships than any stage moment. Frequency builds trust; spectacle builds memories.
  2. Operational friction is feedback.Five groups at once stress-tested every process we had. Where it hurt is exactly where the next version gets better.
  3. Attrition is data.Founders rarely left because the content failed them. They left where scheduling and follow-through collided, so those are the systems we fix.
  4. Pilots prove concepts. Products deserve real builds.Our duct-taped portal proved that founders and staff need one. The next step is a dedicated developer and a real build.
  5. Leave the playbook better than you found it.The matches, the Mondays, the bugs: all of it is written down, so the next cohort starts ahead of where we did.

Off to build the next version. Thank you to everyone who showed up.
